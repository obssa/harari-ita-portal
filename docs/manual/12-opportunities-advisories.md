# Opportunities and advisories

This chapter is for the Agency staff who publish tenders, calls, vacancies and internships (procurement officers)
and for those who publish safety advisories (communications editors). Each section says who may do it. An Amharic
version will follow once the Agency has reviewed this English text.

Opportunities are under **Opportunities** in the admin. Advisories are posts, under **Content → Posts**.

## 1. An opportunity

Who may do it: procurement officers and administrators.

1. Put the files in the library first (**Content → Documents**): the notice, the bid document, the terms of
   reference, any forms. Give each a title in every language it exists in.
2. Open **Opportunities → New opportunity**. In each language tab write the title, a short summary, the details,
   **who may apply** (one requirement per line), **how to apply** and switch on **Published** in each finished
   language. Leave **Outcome** empty for now.
3. On the right choose the **type** (tender, call for proposals, vacancy or internship), the **reference number**
   as printed on the notice, the directorate that issues it and the pillar.
4. Under **Dates** (in Ethiopia's time zone): **Opens** is optional (empty means open from publication); the
   **Deadline** is required. A tender may also have the date the **bids are opened**.
5. Under **Applying**: for a vacancy, the number of **positions** and the kind of **employment**; then, if
   applications are made online, the **https** address, or the Agency's official **email** (never a personal one).
6. Under **Documents**, choose the library files in the order people should read them. Only public documents are
   shown on the site.
7. Save. The page that opens has the **log** underneath.

The site shows the time left, and says "Closing soon" in the last seven days. When the deadline passes the
opportunity moves to the **archive** by itself within five minutes: it stays readable, with its documents and log,
below the open ones.

## 2. The log: addenda, questions, extensions, cancellation

Who may do it: procurement officers and administrators.

On the opportunity's page, under **Addenda and questions**, choose **Publish an entry**. The entry is published at
once, with today's date and the next number, in every language you wrote. It **cannot be deleted later**, only
corrected, so read it before publishing.

- **Addendum:** a change to the documents. Write what changed; choose the revised document from the library if
  there is one.
- **Clarification:** a question a bidder or applicant asked, and the answer. Both are required. Do not write who
  asked.
- **Extension:** a new, later deadline. The opportunity takes it at once; the page says the deadline was extended
  and shows the first deadline too.
- **Cancellation:** the reason is required. The opportunity shows as cancelled and takes no further entries.

Once the log has an entry, the deadline in the form is locked: change it with an **Extension**, so everyone sees
the change. To fix a typing mistake in an entry, use **Correct**: the entry then shows the date it was corrected;
its number, date and deadlines stay as published.

When the result is known (for example the award of a tender), write it in **Outcome** in each language and save.

## 3. An advisory

Who may do it: communications editors and administrators (only they may make a post an advisory or change its
severity). Translators translate advisories as any post.

1. Open **Content → Posts → New post** and choose the type **Advisory**.
2. Choose the **severity**:
   - **Info**: good practice and awareness, no action needed straight away;
   - **Advisory**: a known risk, act soon;
   - **Warning**: an active threat, act now.
3. In each language: the title, the summary, the **affected systems** (one per line), the **steps to take** (one
   per line, in order) and, if needed, more details in the text.
4. Publish as any post (see chapter 2).

Advisories appear under **Advisories** on the site (in the News & Media menu), on the homepage with the latest
news, and in their own feed. Each page tells readers to report cyber incidents to **INSA**, the national authority,
and to use the Agency's IT support for problems with the systems it supports. The Agency gives guidance; it does
not handle national cyber incidents.

## 4. Sending an advisory or an opportunity by email

Who may do it: staff who manage campaigns (communications editors).

On the advisory (or the opportunity), choose **Draft an email campaign**. A campaign draft opens, with the text of
each published language and the link to the page, for newsletter subscribers who chose **Safety advisories** (or
**Vacancies, tenders and notices**). Nothing has been sent yet. Read it, choose **Send a test** to yourself, then
send it or set a time, as for any campaign (chapter 6).
